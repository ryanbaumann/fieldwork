I'm Head of DevRel at Google Maps Platform. Across my career in software, product, and solutions engineering, I've focused on one problem: taking new technical capabilities, especially AI, and landing them as reliable software in real customer workflows.

My team works across product, engineering, UX, technical writing, and field teams. Recent work includes [Code Assist](https://developers.google.com/maps/ai/code-assist), [agent skills](https://github.com/googlemaps/agent-skills), task-based agentic evals, an AI-driven Voice of Developer program, and the [Geo Architecture Center](https://developers.google.com/maps/architecture).

I stay close to the code: writing the first evals for a new agent, debugging retrieval traces, or shipping a reference app to see where a workflow breaks under real constraints. Getting durable value from AI follows a simple loop: find a bottleneck that repeats, ground the system in live context, grade it against real tasks, and ship what teams can actually run. I've written about that operating model in [DevX Is a Growth Function](/writing/devx-is-a-growth-discipline/).

Before this role, I spent a decade building industry solutions and customer-facing engineering teams across manufacturing, financial services and insurance, logistics, and the public sector. At Google Cloud, I led the product and engineering team for [Intelligent Product Essentials](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/introducing-intelligent-products-essentials-for-manufacturers), an edge-AI and analytics product launched with GE Appliances. Earlier, I built the solutions architecture team for applied document AI at Instabase, joined Mapbox as its first customer-facing engineer (launching Boundaries, on-premises Atlas for regulated deployments, and open-source libraries for Python notebooks and Power BI), and built connected-worksite systems at Caterpillar.

I studied mechanical engineering at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, but before writing software, I raced professionally for Trek-Livestrong and Bahati Foundation. Racing taught me to pace long efforts, measure the right signal, and keep looking for the next useful improvement. I still love the outdoors and pushing myself gravel riding and trail running.
